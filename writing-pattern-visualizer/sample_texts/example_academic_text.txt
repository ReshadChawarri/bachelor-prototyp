Digital writing tools have become a routine part of university study. However, many tools focus on correcting a finished text rather than helping writers notice their own process and recurring structural choices. A visual account of writing patterns may support reflection without presenting one form of writing as universally correct.

This prototype therefore treats sentence and paragraph length as visible traces of composition. In addition, it counts a small, explicit list of academic transitions. These measurements do not explain a writer's intentions; nevertheless, they can provide useful starting points for questions about rhythm, emphasis, and connections between ideas.

The design prioritizes interpretability and local processing. Consequently, every displayed observation can be traced to a simple count or rule. The prototype offers no grade, correction, or generated prose. Instead, students remain responsible for interpreting whether the visible patterns fit their communicative goals.
