Abstract

Academic writing often develops ideas through a sequence of claims, evidence, and interpretation. However, students may not always notice how their sentence rhythm shapes the reader's experience. This prototype explores how visual summaries can make writing patterns easier to inspect.

Introduction

Students frequently revise academic texts by focusing on grammar, citations, or formal requirements. In contrast, they may spend less time observing the structure of their own writing behavior. Therefore, a visualization interface can support reflection by showing sentence length, paragraph length, transition words, and section distribution in a descriptive way.

Methodology

The prototype uses local preprocessing rules to split the text into paragraphs and sentences. It then calculates word counts, sentence lengths, paragraph lengths, and explicit transition words from a predefined academic list. For example, transitions such as however, therefore, and in addition are counted because they make relationships between ideas visible.

Results

The visual overview makes several patterns visible. First, sentence lengths differ across the sample text. Second, paragraph length varies between compact introductory passages and more detailed methodological description. As a result, the dashboard provides multiple entry points for reflection without assigning a score.

Discussion

The interface is intended to encourage interpretation rather than correction. On the other hand, simple rule-based analysis has limitations because headings, abbreviations, and formatting conventions can vary across academic texts. Nevertheless, transparent rules make it easier for students to understand what the prototype detects.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the Writing Pattern Visualizer shows how descriptive metrics, charts, highlighted text, and reflection prompts can support exploration of individual academic writing patterns. Finally, future iterations could add richer interaction while keeping the analysis local and interpretable.
